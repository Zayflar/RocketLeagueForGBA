# Game Boy Advance 3D Rocket League

A high-performance, real-time 3D physics-driven Rocket League clone built for the Game Boy Advance (ARM7TDMI, 16.78 MHz) using **devkitARM** and **libtonc**, featuring full 3D world projection, camera coordinate tracking, elastic boundary physics, and an interactive AI opponent.

## Game Design & Features

- **Double-Buffered VRAM Engine**: Implemented via GBA's Mode 4 page-flipping, rendering to fast EWRAM (`frame_buffer`) and transferring to VRAM during VBlank via 32-bit DMA.
- **3D World Space Camera (Chase Cam)**: Camera coordinates tracking dynamically behind the player's car, tilting and turning as the player drives.
- **3D Physics & Collisions**:
  - Cars and ball are simulated with 3D velocity vectors, gravity, drag, and floor/ceiling/wall elastic reflections.
  - Sphere-to-sphere car-to-ball collision resolves overlapping volumes and transfers momentum elastically (with an extra "pop" force on hits).
- **Vector-Based AI Opponent**: Drives, turns, and jumps to chase the ball and push it towards the player's goal. Calculated via 2D vector cross-product steering angles without costly trigonometric lookup overhead.
- **Goals and Explosions**: Goals on each end of the field detect when the ball crosses the goal line inside the cage. Scoring triggers a camera screen-shake explosion animation.
- **Futuristic Wireframe Arena**: Stadium boundary cage and goal frames rendered as 3D lines projected into camera space.
- **Radar Minimap & Dynamic Shadows**: A real-time 2D minimap is displayed in the corner, and oval drop shadows are cast beneath cars and the ball.
- **Particle System**: Engaging rocket boost particle trails visually enhance the speed mechanics.
- **Smooth Chase Camera**: A dynamic third-person camera that elastically trails behind the car like in real racing games, swinging smoothly around the vehicle when you turn.
- **Dev HUD Overlay**: Displays remaining match time, Blue team vs. Orange team score, speedometer (MPH), and a real-time boost percentage gauge.

## Technical Specifications

- **Math Representation**: 8.8 fixed-point math system (`fixed`, `INT_TO_FP`, `FP_MUL`, `FP_DIV`) for high-speed arithmetic on ARM7TDMI.
- **Normal Transformations**: Normal vectors are calculated directly in camera-transformed space to speed up Backface Culling (`nz >= 0`) and headlight directional shading.
- **Simplification**: High-speed edge tracking scanline rasterizer and Bresenham line drawer.
- **Mesh Optimization**: Three low-poly cars with independent octagonal wheels, UV-mapped body panels, and cached lighting normals. The ball uses an 80-triangle icosphere.

## Compiling and Building

1. Ensure you have the `devkitARM` toolchain installed and paths exported (`DEVKITARM`, `DEVKITPRO`).
2. Run the build script in PowerShell:
   ```powershell
   ./compile.ps1
   ```
   This compiles `main.c`, `engine3d.c`, and `models.c`, links them with `libtonc`, produces `gba_3d.gba`, and automatically launches it in mGBA.

### Car texture and renderer checks

Cars share a procedural 64x64 indexed atlas generated by `init_car_texture`
inside `engine3d.c`. Each 16x32 tile is a panel: hood/deck, side decal,
headlights/grille, rear lamps/exhausts, glass, wheel hub, underbody, plain paint.
Index zero uses the face's lit team color; other texels use the Mode 4 palette.
All three car meshes have explicit UVs and closed cabins. The styling uses original
racing stripes and sporting details inspired by classic Rocket League cars.

Texture scanlines preserve their UV gradients at the viewport edges, use wide
intermediate multiplication, and handle projected edges beyond the reciprocal
lookup table. Near-plane clipping retains source winding for backface culling.

Build with `make`. Run `python3 tests/test_renderer.py` for host-side C
regressions covering all near-plane clipping masks, texture gradients, viewport
cropping, and oversized edges under AddressSanitizer and UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer.
These checks require a host C compiler and do not replace emulator/hardware testing.

### 3D garage and team loadouts

The garage now has a rotating, textured 3D showroom preview and separate blue
(player) and orange (opponent) loadouts. Choose **Octane**, an Octane-inspired
low-poly buggy with a wedge nose, raised cabin, exposed octagonal tyres and rear
wing; **Striker**, a longer, lower sports car; or **Rover**, a tall utility body.
These are code-generated meshes and an original procedural atlas, not ripped
Rocket League assets. Selections apply to matches, training and replay rendering.

- **L/R shoulders**: switch the team being configured.
- **Up/Down**: select body or paint; **Left/Right**: cycle its three options.
- Blue paints: **Blue, Cyan, Purple**. Orange paints: **Orange, Red, Gold**.
- **A, B or Start**: finish and return to the menu. Selections last until reset;
  there is no cartridge save persistence for garage configuration yet.

Team identity remains independent of cosmetic paint. The fixed-point software
renderer uses full 3D transforms, directional lighting, near-plane clipping,
affine UV mapping and per-mesh depth sorting. It has no per-pixel depth buffer;
intersecting geometry can still show painter-order artifacts. Mesh bounds are
validated before accessing rendering scratch buffers, and small face normals
retain their direction for lighting thin spoiler supports.

Regenerate car geometry with `python3 tools/generate_cars.py`, then `make`.
Run `python3 tests/test_garage.py` to check all 18 loadouts over full rotations
with AddressSanitizer/UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer. Set `GARAGE_PREVIEW_DIR` to
retain PPM screenshots. This host test executes the production showroom and
renderer with hardware register stubs; it does not measure GBA frame rate.


## Two-player GBA link mode

Choose **Play → 2 Player Link** on both consoles running the same ROM build.
Connect a GBA multiplayer link cable, or use two linked instances of an emulator
that implements GBA multiplayer serial communication. The cable host is blue
(Player 1); the other console is orange (Player 2). Only two consoles are supported.
The host chooses Soccer/Hockey with Left/Right, then presses A to start once both
screens say **LINK READY**. Each player uses their own controls and camera.

Input packets and the host's match-clock increments are synchronized. An interrupted
connection freezes simulation and displays **WAITING FOR LINK**. Press Start+Select
on either console to leave its match; the other player can use the same combination.
Linked games skip goal replays and local pause; the host starts a rematch with Start
on the results screen. Garage appearance selections remain local to each ROM session.

The protocol has automated two-peer tests with delayed, missing, and corrupted
packets. A physical two-GBA / linked-emulator playtest is still required.

## Controls and achievements

Holding R for aerial control rotates pitch and roll 50% faster. The hockey puck
slides with less drag, rests at its actual half-height, and has a low ice bounce.
Achievements is a six-column, four-row icon grid with 24 unlockable challenges.
Use the arrows to select an icon; its name, objective, and progress appear below.
Unlocked icons gain a gold color and check mark. Rank pips distinguish milestones.

The challenges include 1/10/25/50/100 goals, 1/5/10/25 wins, 1/10/25 hockey goals,
3/15/30/60 seconds of wall driving, completing the tutorial, finishing 1/10/50
matches, winning soccer/hockey/link matches, and winning without conceding.
Goals and wins count in matches, including linked matches for your local team;
training and replays do not count. Unlock notifications appear during play.
Progress saves automatically to SRAM with an interrupted-write recovery copy.
Existing six-achievement saves migrate automatically, including their recorded
progress toward the new tiers. Wall progress saves every half-second. Keep your
emulator's `.sav` file alongside the ROM to retain progress between sessions.

The soccer ball uses a smoothly shaded spherical sprite with 16 animated panel
frames at normal camera distances. The precomputed 64×64 frames stay in ROM,
with no added mesh faces or persistent RAM buffer. Extreme close-ups retain the
near-plane-clipped mesh; physics and hockey rendering are unchanged. Regenerate
the sprite data with `python3 tools/gen_ball_sprite.py`.

## Sound

Quiet navigation and confirmation sounds play in menus. Engine and idle sounds
are removed, including their roughly once-per-second restart. During gameplay,
boost audio plays only while boosting; jumps, impacts, and goals remain event-based.
Menu clicks last 0.1 seconds, confirmation cues 0.2 seconds, both at reduced volume.
Music, pickups, and achievement notifications remain silent. Settings → Sound mutes
all audio. The eight clips occupy about 103 KiB of ROM with no sample RAM buffer.
Impacts retain up to 1.2 seconds and goals their full nearly-six-second tail.
Build and automated checks pass; emulator/hardware listening remains unverified.

Achievement icons are grouped by scoring, wins, hockey, matches played, and wall
progression, with increasing milestones next to each other. Display reordering
preserves existing saved unlocks and progress.

Main-menu music now loops the supplied `music_map_09` clip (about 32 seconds),
at a subdued volume. The archive has no song-title metadata. Music stops when
leaving the main menu, and the Sound setting controls it alongside effects.
Engine/idle sounds remain disabled. Shadows now use neutral grey shades over
wall ramps, including shadows straddling grass and ramp surfaces. The renderer
shares its span-fill routine to preserve fast-memory stack headroom.
